SPACESHIP ARCHITECT — OCTOBER 10, 2026 AUDIT AND RELEASE REPORT
Authorized by #commit. Includes the previously completed October 8–9 local changes.

WHAT WAS FIXED IN THIS PASS

1. Repeated console-preview failure
The common navigation restore loop treated a frozen SIC catalog preview as a live console. It could repeatedly reopen/restore the preview, causing dark layers, displacement and wasted work. Catalog previews now bypass both navigation restoration and live navigation synchronization. Hidden Explore perspectives also stop doing restore work. The fallback check runs every 500 ms instead of 150 ms; normal combat state events still update immediately.
The actual Ripple Reflector card -> Preview Console -> Back to SIC Card route was exercised in the GM ship viewer and left open to verify stability. The preview remained a single frozen dialog and returned to the original SIC card.

2. Routine notifications and important combat alerts
Routine action results use the existing bottom-right notification system. They no longer reserve a strip at the top, resize the page, or move console dialogs around. Important alerts use a separate presentation within that same shared system: a colored overlay at the top with scrolling text, a small dismiss X and a fade after the text crosses the screen. It consumes no layout space.
Critical cases include new detection/hostile lock events, detected incoming missiles, shield collapse, power failure, breaches/open airlocks, intruders and crew exposed to vacuum. Alerts respect the viewer's ship information; incoming missile warnings require a detected missile aimed at that ship. Repeated state packets do not replay the same warning. Ending combat clears combat-only notifications. Reduced-motion users get a stationary timed banner.
Hidden Explore perspectives queue important feedback and restore the notification layer when selected. A previously hidden GM result notification was reproduced during testing and fixed.

3. GM acknowledgments and roll results
Quick Prompt sends and the GM's reward, recharge, private-note, roll-request, resource, library, approval and related character-result paths now use compact corner acknowledgments.
A submitted PC roll gives the GM a compact character/roll/result card, with Dismiss and View Results. The details window preserves dice, difficulty and GM-only Reputation comparisons. Opening the same detail window repeatedly is prevented until it closes.
Persuade uses the normal reddish style without a recognition result, purple for an unsuccessful recognition check, and green when recognition succeeds.
A tiny Cancel beside Open Roll removes only that PC's pending request. The server verifies ownership, records cancellation, rejects cancellation of already submitted rolls and makes repeat cancellation safe.

4. Ship AI
New Ship AIs default to Automatic. This mode can scan when there is no detected hostile and prioritizes breaking hostile locks, then uses the existing legal targeting/weapon policy. A turn with no legal action waits and keeps the AI armed instead of disabling it. A temporarily occupied Bridge also leaves the selected mode armed.
Defense, Offense and Off remain available. Existing explicit Off preferences remain Off. This is combat-policy automation, not permission to autonomously warp away, self-destruct, spend forbidden AU, or operate every strategic system. The existing no-AU constraint and normal turn/dice/delay rules remain.

5. Galaxy map and warp
PC and GM Campaign pages now organize information on the left and the galaxy map on the right. The left column and star details scroll independently on desktop. Small screens stack the sections. Player ships are drawn at half their former size while preserving usable hit targets and labels.
The PC Campaign and Combat headers are more compact; the Combat subheading no longer takes an oversized stacked row.
Review Journey identifies the installed Warp Drive, activation time and travel time. Ordinary drives require explicit fuel-grade quantities, with available stock and range per cell shown. Enter Warp stays disabled until the selected fuel supports a valid quote; the server revalidates the actual departure. The EW drive retains its fixed two Grade S cells, all-AU cost and instantaneous jump after activation.
The reported zero travel time was possible with EW instantaneous travel, not a general calculation that regular drives should use. The test flagship now has Warp Drive 5 installed and EW stored as a spare. A 19.56-light-year preview using one Grade B cell showed 48 seconds activation and 6 hours travel.
Only one Warp Drive may be installed, including EW and powered-off drives. Extra drives may remain in storage. Existing user saves are not silently stripped of components; invalid multi-drive layouts must be corrected before validation/travel. This is the user's digital rule, not a claim that the core rulebook explicitly imposed it.
Review Journey/Enter Warp gold buttons have no black text shadow.

6. Interior movement and console layout
Clicking Move on a PC starship now uses the existing Enlarge/Character focus function, centering and zooming on the character rather than fitting the entire ship.
Shared console headers and maintenance controls are more compact. Fleet panels are bounded so their contents scroll instead of displacing the console.
Hibernation Chamber referenced a nonexistent image name. It now uses the existing generated hibernation console art; an asset-reference regression check was added.
Life Support's instrument layer extended into its separate gravity readout. Its radials, wave display, capacity meters and labels now fit their panel. The gravity/oxygen switches no longer produce a horizontal scrollbar or clipped switches. Animation and controls remain in place.

7. Loading and responsiveness
Cold static text compression now runs through asynchronous gzip instead of gzipSync on the combat server's event loop. The bounded 32 MiB cache, gzip responses, ETags/304s and private-resource restrictions remain.
A local stress sample compressed the 561,678-byte character.js asset twelve times. Synchronous: 105 ms total, 95 ms longest timer gap. Asynchronous: 117 ms total, 14 ms longest timer gap. This is evidence of less main-thread blocking, not a claim that the total compression job or every page load is faster.
The existing optimized radial, console-only warp effect from the October 9 pass is included. Hidden perspective/navigation checks avoid unnecessary repeated console work. Larger architectural rendering changes were not made without evidence they were needed.
