## Bugs fixed and rules enforced

- **Construction EN:** browser confirmation and server link/save now share one calculation. A design must power all installed systems without temporary Engineering staffing. Stored engines do not supply power, extra shields include their additional EN cost, and marking an installed item disabled/offline/pending sale cannot hide its design load. Exactly zero spare EN is allowed. The server validates the normalized saved layout, so an out-of-hull engine discarded during normalization cannot make an invalid ship pass. Invalid saves leave the existing ship untouched.
- **Expanded engine stations:** station bonuses previously multiplied row offsets by a fixed 20. They now use the shared construction-grid width, including rotated stations on an expanded grid.
- **Disabled status:** live EN demand/output consistently recognizes the textual disabled status as well as the disabled flag.
- **Explore backup restoration:** a restored demo could update combat while campaign views kept the previous object. Restore now preserves the destination's demo status, updates the active campaign and keeps the disposable room out of normal campaign storage. Reset Room still creates a fresh default scenario.

The EN admission rule concerns a ship's installed design. Live combat damage and deliberate shutdowns still use their established output rules; no new automatic power-shedding priorities or free emergency power were invented.

## Code cleanup actually made

- One shared EN validation function serves both the construction interface and server; there are no separate rule formulas to drift apart.
- Power Engine prices and hull cost now live in the shared ship catalog. The construction UI reads them there. Power Engine card identifiers are also supplied centrally.
- The fleet's equipment/placement data is in a small shared template module, replacing parallel inventory/placement lists embedded in the campaign API. Stable ship and component IDs are retained.
- Removed unused Rex/Mira demo character definitions that were immediately discarded before room creation. Explore still has Nova and Space Slug.
- Removed obsolete missile SVG generation that ran for all ammunition and was immediately overwritten by the existing WebP artwork. Current artwork, animations and audio bindings are unchanged.
- Added a fixed combat test fleet shared by older timing/damage regressions. Tests of manual damage/recovery no longer accidentally change when Explore gains shields, concealment or automatic repair. Separate browser checks use the actual new ships without this test fixture.

I did not find a reason to replace the shared Starships sheet or console architecture. Reusing it is already a strength of this app. The next maintainability opportunities are a shared market-card renderer and a carefully tested common dice-dialog lifecycle. Those are recommendations, not changes made here: cancellation, ownership and retained-result retries make a broad dice rewrite risky without dedicated work. Large files alone do not prove that the app is slow. No frame-rate or memory improvement is claimed from this pass.

## Decisions made without asking

1. Used an approximately 295–296k equipment/hull budget rather than padding either ship to exactly 300k.
2. Gave both ships 100 Hull HP, shields and Repair Drone 1, with all previous weapon families retained.
3. Made Red Horizon more concealed but weaker in weapons, shields and movement; gave Wayfinder the lower remaining EN because it uses more of its power.
4. Required sufficient design EN with every installed SIC included, independent of crew, damage or shutdown state; kept legitimate live damage rules separate.
5. Updated new/reset Explore templates instead of overwriting personal campaign ships or ongoing saves.
6. Limited cleanup to specific duplication/dead work and verified bugs, preserving established interfaces and controls.
7. Reviewed remaining cards but did not implement or purchase any new SIC family.
