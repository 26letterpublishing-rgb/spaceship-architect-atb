Moving forward I want you to remember the following:
If a function already exists, such as a system to give reverence to a player, and something happens where a unique way to give that player reverence doesn't work, and I ask you to fix it, I don't want you to create a whole new system from scratch.  Always look and see if there is a currently functional way to do something.  When you are fixing something that's not working, don't create a whole new way for it to function.  We have some top-bar notifications that have an "x" button to dismiss them.  We have pop-up prompts that force interaction before they can be dismissed.  We have reminder boxes that pop up in the bottom right.  Don't create new ways to deliver messages or new ways to give experience or rewards.  The fewer systems the players need to learn the better.  Always try to use similar code or interactions rather than create new ones.  (This is why we always have the dice animation play for all dice rolls)

Never remove something unless I specifically ask you to remove it.  You are allowed to alter things, but never get rid of a button or option unless I tell you to.

Always be mindful of how much "empty" space is being displayed at any given time.  Display boxes that take up an entire row should have as much text displayed horizontally as possible, because if you stack the text on top of other text, that means the entire bar is now taller, and that tall bar extends all the way to the left and right sides of the screen, wasting a ton of space.  Whenever possible, keep everything condensed, in multiple columns, and have as little whitespace as possible.  You may need to take screenshots of the display yourself, and look at them to be able to see how much whitespace is being unused.  This has been a constant battle working with this code.  When in doubt, use four columns.

Reputation:
While two ships are connected via hailing request answered, the Persuade Check within the Quick Prompt should turn green or purple.  make it so that this button cannot be removed in Quick Prompt.
If the button is purple, it functions exactly the same way it does currently.
If the GM presses that button while it's green, it will ask the GM to choose NPC Affinity and Bias using the Reputation Modifiers.
The GM will choose between:
Benevolent, Indifferent, and Ruthless.
Virtuous, Indifferent, and Treacherous.
Civil, Indifferent, and Savage.
Powerful, Indifferent, and Weak.
Cunning, Indifferent, and Exploitable.
After these are chosen, the NPC will have a bias that aids or hinders the Persuade Checks.
For example, if the PCs had +2 Ruthless, and +1 Savage, but the NPC has an Affinity towards Benevolent and Civil characters, then the PCs would get a -3 to their Persuade Check.  If the NPC had a Bias towards Benevolent and Civil characters (and an affinity towards Ruthless and Savage) then the PCs would get a +3.  Indifferent simply means the modifiers are not applied.
When the GM chooses one of the three options, the words "Affinity" and "Bias" will appear on the appropriate sides of the decision.
For example (GM clicks on Cunning)
>Affinity< CUNNING INDIFFERENT EXPLOITABLE >Bias<
Clicking indifferent would remove the text and the modifiers.  Clicking Exploitable would swap the words Affinity and Bias and reverse the modifiers.

The GM can also manually adjust these values at any time by viewing starships and simply clicking on the blue highlights.

After this happens, all the PCs crewed on that starship will get an inbox notification that their popularity has increased and their reputation has been modified.
